# Platforms: what each one wants

The post is written once, for the platform where it will work hardest, and then
rewritten - never pasted - for the others.

## The table

| Platform | Length | Form | What matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LinkedIn** (profile) | 900-1,800 characters | a teardown: claim → mechanism → inside fact → action, or a document post (carousel as PDF) | a short first line collapses the post to "see more", and what counts is whether they keep reading after the click; the link goes in the first comment by default, though 2026 reports disagree on the cost of a body link - test it |
| **LinkedIn** (company page) | the same | the same, minus the personal story | reach is a fraction of a personal profile's: the page carries announcements, the person carries opinions |
| **Threads** | 300-600 characters | one thought, first person, practice | no links, only the profile; a long post is rewritten for here, not pasted |
| **X** | one or two posts | the shortest thing in the material, a number in the first line | a link in the post costs reach - put it in a reply |
| **Instagram** | caption 150-300, the image does the work | carousel or a single strong image | image-first: a text post without a picture does not exist here. Five to eight slides, the first two are the hook; built from `carousel-template.html` as PNGs |
| **Facebook Page** | 600-1,200 characters | more narrative, with a photograph | **a personal Facebook profile cannot be scheduled by any tool** - Meta allows Pages only. A personal line is written and pasted by hand |
| **Short video** | 45-60 seconds | hook, three points, what to do | not in this queue: text and images only |

## What we never do anywhere

- Paste the same text into two platforms. The audience overlaps more than people
  think, and a broadcast is recognisable instantly.
- Publish to every channel in the same hour.
- Use hashtags as a reach strategy.
- Open with a greeting or with "excited to announce".

## Comments are half the work

Platforms count a comment far above a like, and a reply pulls the post back into
circulation. So:

- Answer every comment, in the first hour if possible, with a sentence rather than
  a thank-you.
- A real question in the post is what makes comments happen. A generic "thoughts?"
  reliably collects three.
- Comment on other people's posts too, in the same niche, with something that
  stands on its own. It is the cheapest reach there is.

## Reposts from the team

When colleagues repost the face's post to their own network:

- Each one writes from their own work, first person, one to three sentences, under
  about forty words. Not a recap, not "great post", not a question to the author
  as if to a stranger.
- At least one in a set adds disagreement or an uncomfortable nuance. Agreement
  alone reads staged.
- Numbers come from the person reposting, left as a `[placeholder]` until they
  confirm them.
- Never invent a failure to sound humble. Before: a repost draft claimed the team
  had gone a year without results, which was neither true nor believable for a
  company operating for years. After: a mistake story is small, old and real, or
  it is absent.

## Comments you write on other people's posts

- No two comments share a construction. Sameness across comments is the first
  thing that marks them as generated.
- Put in one detail that cannot be generalised: a month, a number of accounts, a
  tool by name. Self-correction and doubt are allowed; perfectly optimised is not.

## Rhythm

- Three posts a week per channel to start. Build the queue a week ahead.
- One thought per post. Three thoughts are three posts.
- A missed slot moves to the next one. There are no urgent posts.
- Review once a month, not once a week: a week of data says nothing.

## How one piece of material becomes five texts

A single thirty-minute recording usually holds:

1. One LinkedIn teardown - the mechanism, in full.
2. One short first-person post for Threads - the single sharpest line.
3. One number post for X - the measurement, nothing else.
4. One carousel - the same mechanism as steps, published twice: as a PDF document
   on LinkedIn and as PNG slides on Instagram.
5. One narrative post for a Facebook Page - the same thing, told as what happened.

Same material, five different shapes, published across two weeks rather than one
day. They are not reposts; a reader who follows two of the channels should not
notice they came from one place.
