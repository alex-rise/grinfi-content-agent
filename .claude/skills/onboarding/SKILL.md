# Onboarding: meet the business before writing a word

One conversation, forty to sixty minutes, by voice and recorded if at all
possible - the recording becomes raw material afterwards. If the person will not
talk, the questions work in writing, but you lose the voice.

**Name the client first.** Everything this skill writes lives in
`business/clients/<client>/`, one folder per business. Take the name from the
request or from the site you are about to read, lowercase, hyphenated.

**Read before you ask.** Their site, their blog, their profile, their store
listing, their last ten posts. Every question whose answer is already public and
that you ask anyway costs you credibility in the first ten minutes. Come to the
call with "I read that you do X, is that still the main thing" rather than "what
do you do".

**Ask about the business, not about the operation.** Never ask how many posts a
week they want - that is a consequence of what there is to say. Never ask what
tone they want - tone is taken off their speech, not off their opinion of their
speech.

If this business already has an onboarding from the outbound kit, sections 1-3
are done: read them from there and start at section 4.

## 1. Money and who buys

1. What do you earn from, and what is the average deal?
2. Who buys - describe your last three clients: who they are, how big, what
   they do.
3. Inside the client company, who decides and who blocks?
4. Is there a segment you want more of and have little of?

## 2. How buying actually happens

5. How did your last five clients arrive - step by step, where did they first
   hear of you?
6. How long from "heard of you" to "paid"?
7. What does a person have to understand or see before they commit?
8. Which questions and objections come up in every single conversation?

## 3. What you prove it with

9. Which numbers can you say publicly - yours and your clients'?
10. Which cases can be told with names, and which only anonymised?
11. What do you know about your market that the rest of it does not?
12. Where do you disagree with what everyone in your niche says?

Question 12 is the most valuable in the whole interview. A position someone
could disagree with is the one thing a generator cannot produce.

## 4. Who will be the face

13. Who stands behind the texts - you, a partner, someone on the team? Several
    is fine, but each gets their own platform, never a shared voice.
14. Will you be on video and speak out loud, or text only?
15. How much time a week will you really spend? Reading and approving is fifteen
    minutes. Recording a thirty-minute conversation is thirty minutes.
16. Who says "publish"? One person, or everything stalls.

## 5. Where you have already spoken

The most important section in the interview. All of the material and all of the
voice come out of it.

17. Are there recordings - webinars, podcasts, talks, recorded client calls,
    internal training videos?
18. What have you published before and where? What did better than the rest -
    numbers, or at least your impression?
19. Are there articles, newsletters or documents you wrote yourself?
20. Threads with clients where you explained something difficult in your own
    words?

If there is nothing at all, ask for one thirty-minute recording on any working
topic. Say plainly what it buys: without it you can write correctly, and correct
is not the same as recognisable.

## 6. What is forbidden

21. What can never be published under any circumstances - client names, numbers,
    suppliers, technology, deals not yet closed?
22. Are there NDAs that limit this?
23. Who do you not want to be compared to or associated with?
24. Topics you stay out of: politics, religion, competitors?

Write the answers into `facts.md` as a red list, in their words. This list is
what makes a client trust the machine, more than any text you write.

## 7. Platforms and languages

25. Where do your buyers sit - where do they read and argue about work?
26. Which of your accounts are alive and which are empty? Links, please.
27. Which languages, for which markets?
28. Is there a scheduler already, and who has access to it?

## Create the folders, do not ask the client to

As soon as the call ends, before you write anything, do this - and do not wait
for the client to ask where things go. **Say it first.**

1. Create `business/clients/<client>/` with the six files from `CLAUDE.md`, each
   one filled with what you have and explicit gaps where you do not.
2. Tell them to make one cloud folder in whatever cloud they already use, with
   four subfolders, and give them the message to read: it is in
   `reference/materials.md`, ready to send as it is.
3. The inbox subfolder is the important one. People will not sort. Asking them to
   is the reliable way to receive nothing.
4. Every file that arrives gets a line in `materials.md` in the same session. A
   file nobody catalogued does not exist: nobody will find it or remember it.

## Ask them to send, after the call

- Links to every recording there is.
- 25-30 real photographs of whoever is the face; 10-15 of each second speaker;
  a few with the team or at work.
- Screenshots of the product or the work, if there is anything to show.
- Two or three texts they wrote themselves - anything, even an email to a client.
- Access to the scheduler, or the decision that they will connect it themselves.

## Immediately after the interview, not later

1. Write the answers into `profile.md`.
2. Build `facts.md`: verified numbers with their source, and the red list.
3. Queue the recordings for mining (`mining` skill).
4. Fill `channels.md`: platforms, languages, who approves.
5. Send the client the one-paragraph message about where to put things.
6. Write two posts from what you already have, with the gaps visible. Showing
   the gaps is the demonstration: it is what a generator cannot do.
